Which spectral band is most effective for mapping submerged coral reefs in clear shallow waters due to its ability to penetrate water?

A: Ultraviolet
B: Blue-green visible
C: Near-infrared
D: Mid-infrared
E: Thermal infrared
F: Microwave
G: X-ray
H: Gamma ray
I: Radio wave
J: Red visible

[3] ASSISTANT
The most effective spectral band for mapping submerged coral reefs in **clear shallow waters** is:

**B: Blue-green visible**

### Explanation:

- **Blue-green visible light** (approximately 450–650 nm) is most effective for underwater mapping because it **penetrates water more effectively** than other wavelengths.
- In clear water, blue and green light can travel deeper than red or near-infrared, allowing for better visibility of coral reefs and other underwater features.
- This is why **remote sensing technologies** like **multispectral or hyperspectral imaging** using blue-green wavelengths are commonly used in **underwater mapping** and **aquatic environmental monitoring**.
- Other options like **near-infrared (C)** or **thermal infrared (E)** are not effective in water, as they are absorbed quickly.
- **Microwave (F)** and **radio wave (I)** can penetrate water but lack the resolution needed for detailed mapping of coral reefs.
- **Ultraviolet (A)**, **red visible (J)**, **mid-infrared (D)**, **thermal infrared (E)**, **X-ray (G)**, and **gamma ray (H)** are either absorbed too quickly or not useful for this purpose.

### Correct
