In epidemiology, a disease described as "sporadic" refers to a disease that is:

A: Constantly present at a baseline level in a specific geographic area
B: Occurring irregularly and infrequently
C: Widespread over several countries or continents
D: Showing a persistent, high prevalence in a population
E: Limited to a specific, isolated outbreak
F: Occurring predictably in seasonal patterns
G: Affecting a large number of individuals simultaneously in a community
H: Transmitted directly from animals to humans
I: Present only as isolated, unconnected cases worldwide
J: Caused by systematic failures in public health

[3] ASSISTANT
The correct answer is: **B: Occurring irregularly and infrequently**

### Explanation:
In epidemiology, the term **"sporadic"** refers to a disease that occurs **irregularly and infrequently** in a population. It is not constant, widespread, or predictable in occurrence. Sporadic diseases are not part of a continuous or epidemic pattern but appear occasionally.
